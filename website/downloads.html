Move your mouse with your hand.

Finger Mouse turns a webcam into a hands-free pointer. It moves the real system cursor and sends a real click when you pinch thumb and index. Tune gesture sensitivity and open your system’s accessibility settings to resize the real cursor.

How it works

Start the app, choose your camera, then point with your index finger to move the real system cursor. Open thumb and index to arm one click; pinch them together to click once. Open them again to re-arm. Use System cursor size to change the actual pointer in your operating system’s accessibility settings. Press Esc or use Stop to end tracking.

Private by design: camera frames are processed on your computer. The app does not send camera video to a server.

Before you download

Finger Mouse moves the system pointer and sends real clicks, so the operating system may ask for camera and Accessibility permissions. On macOS, move the app to Applications first, then enable that same copy in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility and quit and reopen it. If permission is still blocked, remove old Finger Mouse entries and add the app from Applications. Downloads are ad-hoc signed and not notarized; macOS may show a security warning and may ask you to grant Accessibility access again after an update. On Linux, use an X11 session; Wayland may block system-wide input. This is a desktop app; it does not control the system mouse on phones or tablets.
