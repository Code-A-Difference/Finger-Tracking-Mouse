Move your mouse with your hand.

Finger Mouse turns a webcam into a hands-free pointer. Point with your index finger to move, pinch to click, pinch and hold to drag, and raise two fingers to scroll. It runs on your computer and never sends your video anywhere.

How it works

Start Finger Mouse and choose Start tracking. Point with your index finger to move the real pointer. A quick pinch of thumb and index clicks where the pinch began; pinch and hold to press and drag, then open your fingers to drop. Hold up two fingers (index and middle) and move up or down to scroll. Moving your real mouse pauses hand control, and Esc stops tracking. Everything is adjustable in Settings.

Private by design: the camera picture is processed on your computer. Finger Mouse doesn't need the internet and doesn't send video anywhere.

After downloading

Windows: run the installer. If Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”, check that the publisher is Code-A-Difference, then choose More info → Run anyway. New apps can see this warning even when they're signed, until Microsoft's reputation system knows them.

macOS: drag Finger Mouse to Applications and open it from there. Allow the camera when asked, and turn on Finger Mouse under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility so it can move the pointer, then reopen it. If macOS says it can't check the app, open it with right-click → Open the first time.

Linux: chmod +x FingerMouse-linux-x86_64.AppImage, then run it. Use an X11 session (Wayland blocks apps from moving the pointer). If it doesn't start, install libxcb-cursor0.

Each release lists SHA-256 checksums in SHA256SUMS.txt so you can check your download. Source code, all releases and the setup guide are on GitHub.
